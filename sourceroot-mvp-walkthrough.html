Walkthrough Purpose

This walkthrough explains the current SourceRoot MVP using the Light MVP Bundle. The goal is to show that SourceRoot is not just a static page or graph. It is a structured provenance system for inspecting knowledge before trusting it.

Core message SourceRoot separates knowledge into nodes, assertions, relationships, sources, credibility metadata, identity context, revisions, and import validation. That makes knowledge inspectable before it becomes trusted.
Demo concept The demo uses the concept of light across physical, dictionary, biblical, symbolic, truth, knowledge, guidance, revelation, and visibility contexts.
Node → Assertion → Edge → Source → Credibility → Identity → Revision → Import Validation

Presenter Checklist

What SourceRoot is A provenance engine for inspectable knowledge, not just a content website.
What the demo proves One concept can be validated, inspected, sourced, connected, and reviewed across multiple domains.
Why warnings matter SourceRoot separates safe-to-preview data from fully reviewed public-release-ready data.
Why assertions matter Claims are inspectable objects with credibility, support, review, and source context.
Why edges matter Relationships are also claims and need their own trust metadata.
Why this matters for AI AI systems need structured provenance, source boundaries, and citation-ready objects instead of flattened answers.

Simple Talk Track

SourceRoot is a prototype trust layer for knowledge. Instead of flattening information into one answer or one page, it separates knowledge into nodes, assertions, relationships, sources, credibility metadata, identity context, revisions, and import validation.

This demo uses the concept of light because light has physical meaning, dictionary meaning, biblical symbolism, and symbolic relationships to truth, knowledge, guidance, revelation, and visibility.

The important part is not just the word light. The important part is that SourceRoot can show what is being claimed, where it came from, how it connects, how trustworthy it is, what still needs review, and whether the data is safe to import.

That is the larger idea: make knowledge inspectable before it becomes trusted.
