# BibleRoot logo and visual-identity design brief v1

Status: planning only. This document neither selects nor approves artwork.

## Purpose and family context

BibleRoot is a source-verifiable environment for exploring biblical text, original-language evidence, translations, commentary provenance, and connected research without concealing the sources or overstating interpretation.

The current SourceRoot family uses the shared `Root` naming suffix, restrained product marks, readable editorial/interface typography, product-specific accents, clear hierarchy, evidence-first panels, consistent navigation, a shared Root switcher and user menu, and the principle “Machines may discover; humans must be able to verify.” DictionaryRoot, HistoryRoot, and current BibleRoot pages establish the family context; this brief plans a distinct BibleRoot identity without changing those implementations.

BibleRoot should feel rooted, trustworthy, enduring, scholarly, accessible, reverent without denominational exclusivity, technologically modern without feeling synthetic or disposable, connected to SourceRoot yet independently recognizable, appropriate for text/translation/language/commentary/provenance research, serious without institutional distance, and durable for many years.

## Visual territories

No territory below is final artwork.

### A. Rooted Manuscript

- Central concept: an open manuscript, codex, page, verse structure, or textual form becomes or reveals roots.
- Symbolism: source text above; lineage, transmission, and inspectable foundations below.
- BibleRoot connection: directly joins scripture study and textual history. SourceRoot connection: the roots visualize traceable origin and provenance.
- Strengths: immediately relevant, warm, scholarly, and adaptable to a distinctive silhouette. Weaknesses: book/root combinations are common and can become intricate.
- Risks: generic church branding—medium if a cross-like spine appears; generic Bible-app branding—high if rendered as a conventional open Bible; publisher—medium; denomination—low to medium; AI/blockchain—low. Sacred symbolism could be misunderstood if a verse, cross, or manuscript tradition is privileged. Avoid that by using abstract textual structure, no doctrinal emblem, and evidence language that never casts SourceRoot as religious authority.
- Production behavior to test later: simplify to two or three page/root strokes for small and favicon sizes; require an optically corrected favicon, not a shrunk full mark. It should hold in one color, reverse cleanly on dark backgrounds, retain open counters on light backgrounds, and print without hairlines or tonal texture.

### B. Verse Network

- Central concept: scripture lines, verse structures, citation marks, or textual units connect through a restrained provenance network.
- Symbolism: discrete witnesses and references remain distinct while their documented connections are visible.
- BibleRoot connection: evokes verses, parallel translations, commentary anchors, and original-language links. SourceRoot connection: emphasizes inspectable evidence paths rather than hidden synthesis.
- Strengths: modern, system-oriented, flexible for motion and interface use, and more distinctive than a literal book. Weaknesses: abstract at first glance and easy to overcomplicate.
- Risks: generic church branding—low; generic Bible-app branding—low to medium; publisher—low; denomination—low; AI/blockchain—high if nodes dominate. Sacred symbolism could be trivialized by making scripture look like a data graph. Avoid circuitry, glowing nodes, and autonomous-intelligence cues; use citation/line geometry and position the network as provenance, never religious authority.
- Production behavior to test later: reduce to a few connected lines or citation points at small/favicon size. Connections must remain visible in monochrome, against dark and light backgrounds, and in print; no meaning may depend on glow, gradient, animation, or tiny nodes.

### C. Source Seal

- Central concept: a durable archival or verification mark combining text, roots, citation, source, origin, and provenance.
- Symbolism: custody, authenticity, and a checkable record—not theological certification.
- BibleRoot connection: conveys serious stewardship of texts and editions. SourceRoot connection: embodies verification and provenance motifs.
- Strengths: compact, authoritative, excellent for one-color use, print, favicons, and document attribution. Weaknesses: may feel institutional, legalistic, or less welcoming.
- Risks: generic church branding—medium if ecclesiastical forms appear; generic Bible-app branding—low; publisher—high; denomination—medium if heraldic; AI/blockchain—low to medium if treated as a trust badge. Sacred symbolism may be read as an imprimatur. Avoid crosses, crests, official seals, crowns, or “verified truth” language; present it as source verification, not religious authority.
- Production behavior to test later: use a bold outer/inner relationship and minimal internal glyph for favicon size. It must survive one color, knockout on dark, dark ink on light, grayscale, office printing, emboss-like reproduction, and low-resolution display without implying certification.

## Future logo system

The selected future identity must define a primary combined symbol/wordmark, standalone symbol, favicon, application icon, horizontal and compact versions, light- and dark-background versions, one-color and grayscale versions, minimum readable sizes, clear space, accessible contrast, SVG masters, PNG exports, social/profile image, and documented use in navigation, loading states, documentation, print, presentations, and relation to the SourceRoot parent brand. Chunk 14B creates or exports none of these assets.

## Design constraints

The future mark must remain recognizable at favicon size; work without gradients and in one color; avoid fine detail; stay legible in mobile navigation, dark mode, and print; remain durable rather than trend-led; and sit coherently beside DictionaryRoot, HistoryRoot, future EarthRoot, and future Roots.

It must avoid generic clip-art roots, trees, books, flames, doves, globes, crosses, AI circuitry, and blockchain nodes. It must not copy Bible apps, churches, denominations, publishers, or study platforms. Respect biblical text and sacred symbolism. Never imply SourceRoot is a religious authority or that one translation, commentary, denomination, or tradition is universally authoritative.

## SourceRoot family relationship

Shared traits should include a compatible typography family; `NameRoot` wordmark structure and treatment of the `Root` suffix; spacing and proportions; deliberate icon geometry; provenance/verification motifs; hierarchy; navigation, root-switcher, and user-menu integration; and common minimum-size behavior. BibleRoot can be distinct through a future accent color, textual/manuscript/archival or restrained illuminated tone, scriptural-reference motif, and a related but unmistakable icon without denominational or doctrinal cues.

In a family lineup, common baseline, cap height, clear space, stroke logic, symbol footprint, and suffix treatment should make all Roots related. A distinct silhouette and accent should keep BibleRoot from being interchangeable. SourceRoot remains the parent/system identity; sibling marks have equal visual rank, and future Roots can join without copying BibleRoot's subject-specific symbol.

## Preliminary color territories

No final palette is selected, and no current application color changes in Chunk 14B.

### Deep indigo and parchment

- Fit and roles: indigo suggests study and depth; parchment adds textual warmth. Candidate primary: indigo; secondary/background: parchment; accent: a restrained lighter indigo or neutral.
- Performance: strong light-mode contrast when dark indigo is used on pale parchment; dark mode needs a near-black indigo ground and sufficiently light text. Grayscale should retain a wide value gap; print is practical if parchment becomes paper/low tint; favicon works with solid indigo and a light glyph.
- Risks: moderate denominational and generic Bible-app associations, decorative/outdated risk if parchment is textured, and ornamental/luxury risk if paired with gold-like effects. Family compatibility is strong if the accent is used within shared neutral interface structure.

### Midnight blue and warm gold

- Fit and roles: midnight blue conveys seriousness and reliability; warm gold signals illumination and valued source material. Candidate primary/background: midnight; secondary neutral: warm off-white; accent: gold used sparingly.
- Performance: excellent dark-mode potential, but gold/text combinations require measured WCAG contrast; light mode should use midnight for text and gold for non-text accents. Grayscale must not rely on gold hue; print needs non-metallic definitions; favicon can be a simple gold-on-midnight two-tone or one-color variant.
- Risks: medium denominational, generic Bible-app, decorative, and luxury associations; high ornamental risk if metallic gradients appear. It can fit SourceRoot when gold denotes provenance highlights, not prestige or divine authority.

### Charcoal and restrained burgundy

- Fit and roles: charcoal is archival and sober; burgundy evokes historical bindings without literal illustration. Candidate primary/text: charcoal; accent: restrained burgundy; backgrounds: warm pale neutral; secondary: mid-gray.
- Performance: robust light mode, grayscale, and print; dark mode needs softened off-white text and a brighter accessible burgundy accent. A one-color charcoal favicon is strong, though two-color small marks need contrast testing.
- Risks: low generic Bible-app risk, medium denominational/publisher risk, and some outdated or institutional risk. Restraint avoids decoration and luxury. Family compatibility is good if burgundy remains a product accent rather than replacing shared neutrals.

### Dark evergreen and aged ivory

- Fit and roles: evergreen suggests rooted endurance and living study; aged ivory adds quiet textual warmth. Candidate primary: evergreen; secondary/background: ivory; accent: a lighter botanical or neutral tone.
- Performance: strong light-mode contrast with sufficiently dark green; dark mode requires a separate deep-green surface and light ivory text. Grayscale values must be tested because green can collapse; print and favicon behavior are strong with flat colors.
- Risks: low to medium denominational and generic Bible-app risk, low luxury risk, and medium outdated/decorative risk if botanical ornament or distressed ivory is used. It aligns with the Root family but must not become a generic tree/environment identity.

## Typography planning

- Wordmark: dignified, distinctive, compact, and optically balanced with the family suffix; historical resonance without faux-antique or ecclesiastical display conventions.
- Application headings: confident editorial hierarchy with modern screen clarity. Biblical text: comfortable long-form rhythm, clear punctuation, verse numbers, italics, quotations, and diacritics without implying a particular edition.
- Original-language text: proven Unicode coverage and shaping for polytonic Greek and pointed/unpointed Hebrew, correct right-to-left behavior, combining marks, cantillation/accents, and mixed-script citations. No visual style should suggest one manuscript or tradition is normative.
- Translation comparison: comparable metrics, stable line height, distinguishable labels, and legible differences without typographic ranking. Commentary: long-form readability and clear separation from biblical text.
- Source/provenance metadata: compact but readable; identifiers and hashes require an unambiguous technical/monospace companion with distinct `0/O`, `1/l/I`, punctuation, and wrapping. Mobile navigation needs strong small-size legibility; print/presentation use needs reliable weights, embedding/licensing, and broad character coverage.

Candidate evaluation must test readability, dignity, historical resonance, modern usability, multilingual/Greek/Hebrew support, punctuation and citation clarity, the distinction between interface and scripture, and small-size performance. No font—commercial or otherwise—is distributed, embedded, or committed in this stage.

## Weighted evaluation rubric (100 points)

Score each criterion from 0-5, where 0 is absent/harmful, 1 is poor, 2 is weak, 3 is acceptable, 4 is strong, and 5 is exceptional. Weighted score = weight × score / 5.

| Distinctiveness | 12 | Recognition among research and scripture products. | Memorable silhouette without explanation. | Interchangeable book, root, or badge. |
| SourceRoot-family consistency | 10 | Belonging through structure and system behavior. | Clearly a sibling at a glance. | Either copied sibling or disconnected identity. |
| BibleRoot relevance | 12 | Connection to text, language, translation, commentary, and provenance. | Multiple BibleRoot functions cohere without literalism. | Only “religion” or only “technology.” |
| Trust and seriousness | 11 | Appropriate stewardship and evidence posture. | Calm, credible, precise, welcoming. | Flippant, sensational, authoritarian, or institutional. |
| Legibility | 10 | Recognition across wordmark and interface sizes. | Clear counters, spacing, and wordmark at tested minima. | Fine detail, ambiguous letters, collapse on mobile. |
| Favicon performance | 9 | Recognition in the smallest high-frequency context. | Distinct at 16/24/32 px without relying on color. | Full mark merely shrunk; noisy or generic blob. |
| Monochrome performance | 8 | Reproduction independent of effects. | Meaning and hierarchy survive one ink and knockout. | Gradient, transparency, or hue carries the concept. |
| Accessibility | 9 | Contrast, recognition, cognition, and assistive contexts. | Tested contrast, non-color distinction, clear labeling. | Low contrast or color-only differentiation. |

## Required boundaries

- Chunk 14B does not approve a final logo.
- No generated image is automatically accepted as production identity.
- Final artwork requires a separate governed stage and human comparison and selection.
- No current BibleRoot interface or branding asset, favicon, application icon, or application color is replaced during this stage.
- No commercial font file or any font file is distributed, embedded, or committed.
- This brief remains separate from the relationship dataset, migration 019, relationship API, runtime provisioning, and relationship evidence.
- This brief does not establish trademark clearance.
- Sacred symbolism must be handled respectfully.
- BibleRoot must not imply that SourceRoot is itself a religious authority.
- BibleRoot must not imply that one translation, commentary, denomination, or tradition is universally authoritative.
