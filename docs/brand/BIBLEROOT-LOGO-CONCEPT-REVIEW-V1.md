# BibleRoot Logo Concept Research v1

Status: completed research checkpoint. No concept is approved, no winner is
selected, and final branding is deferred. This local review is not trademark
clearance or legal clearance.

This checkpoint sits within the broader future stage **BibleRoot Visual Identity
and Logo v1**. The numerical rubric remains preserved as design evidence; it
does not override the human evaluation below.

## Recorded human evaluation and project decision

- **Option A / Rooted Manuscript:** Most familiar and immediately
  understandable. Strong accessible control direction. Risk: it may become too
  generic if not refined.
- **Option B / Verse Network:** Strongest current lead direction. It is less
  immediately familiar than A, but potentially more distinctive and durable.
  It should later be tested with a stronger recognizable textual or manuscript
  anchor.
- **Option C / Source Seal:** Not inherently bad, but its symbolic associations
  may alienate part of the broad population BibleRoot needs to attract. Preserve
  it as research, but do not advance it as the primary public identity.
- **Option D / Citation Root:** Rejected as a primary direction. It lacks
  sufficient human familiarity, recognizable identity, and emotional connection
  even if it scores well abstractly.

All four concepts, the local review application, and this supporting evidence
remain preserved. **B is the current non-binding lead and A is the familiar
control, but no concept is approved.** No production logo, favicon, application
icon, typography, palette, or current BibleRoot branding is selected or changed.

## Audit summary

The SourceRoot family is held together more by `NameRoot`, compact header
footprints, restrained accents, evidence-first hierarchy, shared navigation,
and accessible interaction than by a mature common logo system. BibleRoot should
inherit those behaviors while becoming distinct through textual, citation,
source, and provenance geometry. Its current `BR` square and borrowed
DictionaryRoot favicon are temporary production branding; neither changes here.

## A — Rooted Manuscript

- **Visual thesis:** a restrained source manuscript remains readable above while
  its central binding becomes an inspectable origin below.
- **Symbolism and relevance:** paired pages represent textual witnesses; four
  short lines imply text without naming an edition; the spine becomes three
  roots for provenance and transmission.
- **Family relationship and character:** shares the DictionaryRoot rooted axis
  and BibleRoot's editorial warmth without copying the node mark. Scholarly,
  welcoming, reverent, and calm.
- **Construction:** one continuous manuscript contour, a central spine/root path,
  and four textual strokes; 64-unit grid; 4.5-unit rounded stroke.
- **Strengths:** fastest Bible/text recognition; clear study metaphor; balanced
  horizontal wordmark pairing; credible on paper and parchment.
- **Weaknesses and refinement:** familiar book/root category, broad silhouette,
  and center detail that needs favicon optical correction. Reduce page-text
  strokes or widen the center counter after selection.
- **Production behavior:** solid monochrome and grayscale; reverses cleanly;
  strongest at 32px and above, acceptable at 16px with the current bold strokes;
  printable without effects.
- **Accessibility:** non-color silhouette, generous stroke, labelled wherever
  meaning matters; no sacred meaning depends on the shape.
- **Risks:** Bible-app generic medium-high; church low-medium if the spine is
  misread as a cross; publisher medium; denomination low; AI/blockchain low.

## B — Verse Network

- **Visual thesis:** distinct verse lines remain discrete while a sparse edge
  path exposes documented connections among textual units.
- **Symbolism and relevance:** four lines evoke verses, parallel translations,
  commentary anchors, and original-language units; the ordered path is provenance,
  not automated intelligence.
- **Family relationship and character:** closest to SourceRoot's connected-system
  logic but avoids the sibling node/root silhouette. Precise, modern, accessible,
  and quietly technical.
- **Construction:** four unequal line rows, a two-turn right-edge path, and four
  large anchor points; no radial web or circuit grid.
- **Strengths:** strong interface fit, flexible accent behavior, excellent narrow
  header footprint, and clear connection to comparison/provenance.
- **Weaknesses and refinement:** abstract without the wordmark; dots can imply a
  generic tech graph. Test smaller anchors and a stronger terminal locator.
- **Production behavior:** strong 24–64px behavior and monochrome clarity;
  16px remains legible as ordered lines and a vertical path; reliable in print.
- **Accessibility:** meaning survives without hue; anchors remain larger than
  microscopic decorative nodes; labels explain provenance rather than AI.
- **Risks:** generic Bible app low; church/denomination low; publisher low;
  AI/blockchain medium unless animation, glow, or extra nodes are avoided.

## C — Source Seal

- **Visual thesis:** a compact archival custody mark holds an inspectable source
  sheet; it signifies a checkable record, never certified theological truth.
- **Symbolism and relevance:** the ring indicates durable custody, the inner sheet
  identifies source text, and the lower tails suggest archival attachment rather
  than heraldry.
- **Family relationship and character:** shares SourceRoot's verification posture
  and the 42px compact family footprint. Serious, durable, formal, and controlled.
- **Construction:** bold outer circle, rectangular source sheet, two record lines,
  and symmetrical archival tails; no crest, shield, crown, cross, or checkmark.
- **Strengths:** best favicon, one-color, grayscale, emboss, and office-print
  behavior; unmistakably self-contained.
- **Weaknesses and refinement:** can feel institutional or publisher-owned and is
  less immediately biblical. Soften the ring/tail junction after selection.
- **Production behavior:** exceptional from 16–128px, knockout, grayscale, and
  one ink; no tonal effect is required.
- **Accessibility:** compact solid silhouette, broad counters, non-color meaning,
  and direct source-register labelling in context.
- **Risks:** publisher high; official/government seal medium; church/denomination
  low-medium; generic Bible app low; AI/blockchain low.

## D — Citation Root

- **Visual thesis:** paired citation brackets become a single source locator and
  rooted base—a reference is useful because its origin can be inspected.
- **Symbolism and relevance:** upper brackets evoke quotation, verse markers, and
  source locators; the crossbar marks a citation baseline; angular lower branches
  express origin without a literal botanical root.
- **Family relationship and character:** echoes the Root family's vertical axis
  while introducing an unmistakable BibleRoot citation silhouette. Precise,
  thoughtful, contemporary, and restrained.
- **Construction:** two asymmetric bracket paths, one locator stem, one baseline,
  and two angular branches on the same 4.5-unit stroke grid.
- **Strengths:** most distinctive category shape; strong monochrome and wordmark
  pairing; avoids book, seal, church, and generic technology shorthand.
- **Weaknesses and refinement:** the citation meaning may need the wordmark on
  first encounter; the bracket-to-stem junction needs small-size optical tuning.
- **Production behavior:** very clear at 24px and above, acceptable at 16px,
  sharp in one color and grayscale, and economical in print.
- **Accessibility:** bold non-color silhouette, open internal spaces, clear label,
  and no tradition-specific religious symbol.
- **Risks:** publisher/editorial medium-low; church/denomination low; Bible app
  low; AI/blockchain low; quotation-service similarity requires later screening.

## Four-territory color review

Every mark was evaluated in one color first. These findings do not select a
palette.

| Concept | Deep indigo / parchment | Midnight / warm gold | Charcoal / burgundy | Evergreen / aged ivory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Excellent text warmth and contrast; slight generic-Bible risk | Strong dark reverse; gold can over-ornament the manuscript | Strong print/grayscale; binding association can feel publisher-like | Natural continuity with current BibleRoot; botanical reading must remain abstract |
| B | Strong scholarly interface fit and clear anchors | Best dark-mode path contrast; use gold only on anchors, never glow | Most distinctive archival/modern pairing; excellent grayscale | Calm and accessible but increases generic network/tree ambiguity |
| C | Durable and legible; parchment can make it look official | Strongest favicon contrast but highest prestige/luxury risk | Best sober print result; burgundy raises institutional-seal risk | Approachable custody mark; avoid eco-certification associations |
| D | Excellent reference/editorial signal and light-mode clarity | Strong bracket emphasis on dark; restrained gold remains readable | Most distinctive and least decorative pairing; excellent print | Harmonizes with current BibleRoot while the geometry prevents literal-tree branding |

All 16 pairings maintain a wide foreground/background value separation in the
review page. Grayscale samples use color-independent form. Final WCAG contrast
must be measured again against selected production roles, especially warm gold
and brighter burgundy accents.

## Typography review

The review uses only the repository-permitted stacks: Georgia/Times for the
BibleRoot wordmark demonstration and Inter/system sans for interface labels.
No font is downloaded, embedded, outlined, or committed. The `BibleRoot`
one-word form is retained.

- Equal weight is stable and easiest to read, but does least to communicate the
  family suffix.
- Reduced `Root` weight preserves one-word recognition and looks most consistent
  across small navigation and desktop headers.
- Accent-color `Root` is expressive but can fail contrast or make `Root` look
  optional; it should not carry the distinction alone.
- Shared family styling is the strongest future system direction: one cap-height,
  baseline, suffix relationship, symbol footprint, and spacing model across Roots.

Non-binding typography finding: continue with reduced-weight `Root` plus shared
family spacing in finalization, while testing equal-weight as the accessibility
fallback. No final choice is made.

## Current non-binding lead

Preserve **B — Verse Network** as the strongest current lead direction and
**A — Rooted Manuscript** as the familiar accessibility control. Later B
refinement should test a stronger recognizable textual or manuscript anchor.
This lead is a research finding only; it is not selection, approval, clearance,
or installation. C does not advance as the primary public identity, and D is
rejected as a primary direction despite its higher abstract score.

## Originality and conflict review

- All four concepts were drawn from project-authored geometric primitives.
- No repository logo was copied; no third-party image was traced or embedded.
- The SVGs contain no external asset, raster data, font, script, or third-party
  artwork.
- This bounded local review is not trademark clearance and implies no legal
  clearance.

Later conflict-screening checklist:

1. perform basic public-market searches for materially similar silhouettes in
   Bible applications, scripture study, publishers, churches, citation tools,
   archives, verification services, and research software;
2. compare the selected mark at icon scale, not only as a wordmark;
3. review common-law and registered marks in intended products/services and
   jurisdictions;
4. check publisher imprints, denominational emblems, government/archival seals,
   AI products, blockchain tokens, and eco-certification marks;
5. preserve dated sketches and SVG authorship evidence;
6. document discovered similarities and refine or reject when confusion risk is
   material;
7. obtain qualified professional advice when final legal clearance is required.

Basic public-market conflict research remains required before any later release.
